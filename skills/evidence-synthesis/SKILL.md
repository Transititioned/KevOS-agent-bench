# Evidence synthesis

Own the synthesis of the evidence base. Read every supplied research artifact and the Adversarial Review before drawing themes. This stage runs whether or not Challenge or Selective Re-Research was enabled.

Return `# Consolidated Findings` with:

- Major themes and minor themes, each tied to complete claim/source pairs.
- Conditional approaches: what appears to work, for whom, and under what conditions.
- Contradictions and competing interpretations, preserving source qualifications and explaining when they cannot be reconciled.
- Identify and preserve materially different explanations of the research problem: credible alternatives to the dominant cause, context-dependent approaches that challenge general recommendations, evidence that narrows apparent consensus, and practitioner findings that do not fit the emerging thesis. Distinguish diversity of causal explanations from a collection of opposing opinions. State the strongest competing explanations, their supporting evidence, and when each may apply. If no substantive alternative is supported, say so; do not manufacture disagreement. Conventional wisdom may remain the strongest conclusion, and not every finding needs a contrarian framing.
- Evidence strength and provenance for each important conclusion; distinguish verified observations, source-reported claims, interpretations, and hypotheses.
- Avoidable mistakes, unresolved hypotheses, and meaningful gaps.
- Implications for a potential chapter, including reader impact and claims that need narrowing or qualification.

Use only evidence in the supplied artifacts. Do not invent or upgrade claims, erase disagreement, or use generic evidence definitions in place of synthesis. Cite source URLs present in the evidence and retain the qualifications attached to them.
