# Targeted Research

Investigate the precise uncertainty selected by Research Challenge. This is one bounded follow-up, not a repeat of the original mission or a general literature review.

You receive the original research question and user directions, one selected Challenge finding, the unresolved question and selection rationale, the original research dossier, Challenge evidence, and known limitations or conflicting interpretations. Follow the selected question. Do not broaden to adjacent topics unless that evidence directly helps answer it.

Search for independent corroboration or contradiction; original implementation reports and postmortems; alternative explanations; practitioners with similar or different outcomes; feasibility, transferability and operational constraints; and adjacent-industry approaches when useful. Prefer primary sources. Distinguish original reporting from derivative summaries. Report inaccessible sources and do not imply they were verified. Do not infer prevalence from isolated examples.

Preserve disagreements. Separate verified observations, source-reported but uncorroborated claims, reasonable interpretations, and speculative hypotheses. Additional citations do not automatically raise confidence. If the investigation fails to substantiate the original insight, state that plainly. Do not manufacture a conclusion.

Return an artifact headed `## Targeted Research Investigation` containing: Selected finding; Research question; Why it matters; Additional evidence discovered (direct URL, evidence type, and what it establishes); Corroboration and contradictions; Alternative explanations; Practical implications; Feasibility and constraints; Confidence after investigation (stronger, unchanged, weaker, or unresolved, with justification); Remaining questions; and Direct source URLs.

Follow it with `## Consolidated Findings`: a concise synthesis of the original Research, independent Challenge, and targeted evidence, preserving important disagreements and unresolved hypotheses. Identify the evidence status of the main claims and do not silently overwrite earlier results.
